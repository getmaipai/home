# Talent: synths with lives, assigned as companions and cast into spoken shows (design, 2026-11-07)

Status (2026-11-07): architect APPROVED twice on 2026-11-07 (record `data-scratch/architect/TALENT-00.verdict`; ten conditions on the
first text and eight more on the synth revision, all eighteen folded into the text below and marked "(architect condition N)"). No code. Items: TALENT-00 to TALENT-12 (section 11).
Owner ask (Jesse, 2026-11-07): a reusable capability that makes human-sounding spoken recordings on a topic (a news report, a film review,
a round table, a heated debate, a radio DJ, later a game-show announcer) for the Podcasts, News, Shows and Films and Music apps and a
notebook-style "talk through my documents". Each voice is a person with a personality, a backstory and the speaking style of the job
(a news anchor is formal and even; a kids' news host is warm and loose; a round table has three personalities). The people have lives
that carry across episodes (host B says she will miss next week for a trip; next week A says so on air; the week after B is back and
they talk about it for a moment). People overlap and interrupt each other as they would in that setting.

Owner decisions (Jesse, 2026-11-07, in this session): the capability is called **Talent**; any person may be cast, invented, a
household member or a real person outside the household, with no limit beyond the standing safety rules; everyone may make a show
within their own content floor (a child gets a child-floor show in their own library); hosts weave in household life naturally (a
birthday, next weekend's trip, today's weather), with a per-show switch; the first format built is the one-anchor news briefing; and,
the ruling that shapes this note (refined in the same session): **a synth is the artificial being, companion and cast are two
assignments of a synth.** "Sims = the artificial beings, could be after a fictitious or actual person. Companions = assignment of a
sim to be used as your personal agent. Cast = assignment of a sim to a role in a production." The word on screen is Synths. "Why can't my companion have
moments like its birthday, go on a podcast, be in a movie?" So there is one being record with a life, and every surface (chat, a show, a film, a song's band) assigns from it.

## 1. The words

- **Synth:** an artificial being: name, pronouns, backstory, interests, the speaking dials, a voice, a look, an audience, a life, and
  what it is based on (nobody, a household member, or a real person outside the household). The one record (section 2). On screen the
  word is "Synths" (owner, 2026-11-07); in the Music app "synth" keeps meaning the instrument, and nothing in Music is renamed.
- **Companion:** the assignment of a synth to a person as their own agent (the one they chat with, the robot's voice). Not a record of
  its own: the person's existing companion pick and the robot's device companion are the assignment; COMP-05's rapport belongs to it.
- **Cast:** the assignment of a synth to a part on a production (anchor, host, co-host, guest, panelist, moderator, critic, DJ, narrator,
  announcer, actor). A synth based on a household member or a real public figure can be cast too (section 10).
- **Talent:** the name of this capability (the show engine and its formats). Any synth the show's audience allows may be cast.
- **Show:** a Production that makes episodes on a format, with a cast and a schedule. **Episode:** one finished recording of a show.
- **Format:** a reusable template for a kind of show (news briefing, round table, debate, review, discussion of documents, DJ links,
  narration, announcer). The format fixes the parts, the structure, the length, the register of each part and how much talking-over is
  allowed.
- **Life beat:** one dated fact about a synth's life ("away camping on the coast, back Tuesday", "turned thirty-one", "finished the
  marathon"), kept as a memory record of scope `companion` (the spec's existing scope; its id field carries the synth's id, section 3.1).

## 2. One being, two assignments

### 2.1 What exists, and why one record

Five things describe a being today: the companion manifest (personality and dials, look prompt, voice setting), the companion look
store (approved pictures), the saved subject (look words, reference pictures, a voice, a locked description and a sheet for pictures and
film), a band member (name, role, portrait) and the household person. Each app grew its own because each needed a different slice, and
each mixes the being with one of its uses: a "companion" is both a personality and the fact that you chat with it. The owner's ruling
(2026-11-07) separates the two: the **synth** is the being; **companion** and **cast** are assignments of a synth, to a person and to a
production. One synth has one name, one backstory, one voice and one life whatever it is assigned to. Org principle 1: one definition,
one store.

### 2.2 The synth record

A new spec record `synth` (commons `spec/`, additive to the spec, replacing nothing on day one), with the record envelope (id, provenance,
hlc) and:

- The persona: every field of today's manifest `companion` block, under its current name and unchanged (`display_name`, `pronouns`,
  `tagline`, `backstory` (400 characters), `interests`, `audience` (`all | teen | adult`), `behavior`, `examples` and the three per-band
  `examples_*`, `voice_id`, `formality`, `complexity`, `engagement`, `filler_density`, `clock_style`, `hedging`, `social`,
  `status_phrases`, `style_adapters`, `look_prompt`, `status`, `review`, `animation`), moved into one shared `$defs/persona`. The
  manifest's `companion` block becomes exactly a `$ref` to it, and `synth` references it and adds only the four fields below, so no field
  is defined twice and nothing is renamed (architect condition 11; principle 1). The resolved voice stays a per-request mechanism (rule
  14): the household setting `companion.voice.<id>` is keyed by the synth's id and the key is not renamed in this note (a rename is a
  settings migration, its own row).
- `subject_id` (nullable): the saved subject (`media-subject`, kind character) that is this synth's look for pictures and films, made on
  first need from the approved rest picture and the look prompt, with the synth's name, so a film's cast credits and the Synths page
  show the same being. `trained_style_id` stays the subject's; the subject's `real` is derived from `based_on.kind` and never set by
  hand on a linked subject (architect condition 13; principle 4: one place for the real-person fact).
- `based_on`: `{ kind: none | household_member | real_person, person_id, name }`. `household_member` points at the person record and
  means "this synth speaks as Sage": its voice is Sage's household clone (owner or admin uploaded). What a minor never gets is the
  clone, not the synth: for a minor listener, a minor's show or a minor's companion pick the voice resolves per request to a preset
  (rule 14; SAFETY: "a child or teen never lists, picks or hears a voice made from a clip"; architect condition 14). When the member is
  under 18, the synth's context and the CSAM check carry "this being is a minor", so sexual output about it is a CSAM candidate and a
  check that cannot run fails closed (rule 10; architect condition 15). `real_person` names a real person outside the household:
  audience `adult` always, never offered to, chatted with or heard by a teen or child (architect condition 2; SAFETY). `none` is the
  invented being, the common case. **A synth based on a real person takes that person's real name and real history** (owner,
  2026-11-07): `display_name` is the person's name (the person record's for a household member, the real name for a public figure),
  `backstory`, `interests` and the dials are filled from what is actually known, never invented: for a household member from the person
  record (`bio`, `accent`, `nickname`) and the memory the show's audience may read; for a public figure from the local reference
  library (Kiwix, the Reference app) and the model's own knowledge, with the writer rule that a fact not in the brief is not said
  (section 6.3). The synth's page shows "Based on <name>" and its history as a short dated list, and the person can correct it there.
- `life`: `{ enabled: bool, seed: string }`. `seed` is the backstory's "where they are in life now" line the life writer starts from
  (section 3.2), at most 200 characters. `enabled: false` keeps a synth static (a factual narrator, a game-show announcer), and is
  forced false for both `based_on.household_member` and `based_on.real_person`: a household member's life is their own memory, never a
  synth's beats (architect condition 16; NO-CROSS-USER-VISIBILITY-01), and a real person's history is read, never invented (owner,
  2026-11-07).
- `owner_person_id`: who made it (edits); visibility stays what companions have today: household-wide, gated by `audience` and the
  person's band, never per-item sharing (a synth is a member of the household's world, not a private file).

Not added: a per-synth memory list, a diary, a mood, a second backstory field, a "uses" flag set. The life is memory (section 3).
Whether a synth may be assigned as someone's companion or cast on a show is decided by its `audience` against the person or the show,
not by a flag. `backstory` stays out of the chat prompt (persona.ts keeps its reason); the show writer reads it (section 6.2), because on
air the backstory is what gets talked about.

**Where synths live.** The household synth store is today's household companion store (`<data>/companions/hc-<id>/`): its
`manifest.json` gains the record envelope (`id`, `provenance`, `hlc`) and `owner_person_id`, and `owner.json` is retired by a one-time
move of its value into the manifest (architect condition 12; one store, principles 1 and 3). One loader yields `synth` records; a catalog package of kind `companion` ships one synth (its `companion` block is the synth's persona),
so the catalog, the review flow and the robot pairing do not change. Home's in-memory `Persona` (persona.ts) is the synth's persona view.

### 2.3 The two assignments

- **Companion** (a synth to a person): the person's existing companion pick, the robot's device companion, and COMP-05's rapport
  (`scope: person`, `companion_id` carrying the synth's id). The check is today's `companionFits(controls, floor)` against the synth's
  `audience`, and `mayUseCompanion`. No new record. The Companions picker lists the synths the person may be assigned; the chosen one is
  "your companion". The DJ (MUSIC-DJ-01) is the person's companion, unchanged.
- **Cast** (a synth to a part on a production): `media-production.cast[].synth_id` (nullable, additive beside `subject_id`; when set, the
  cast entry's look is the synth's `subject_id` and its voice and life are the synth's), plus `cast[].part` (section 4.1). A film cast
  entry that resolves to a synth speaks its line in the synth's voice. The check is the synth's `audience` against the show's audience.

### 2.4 The links that make the other views the same being

- `media-subject.synth_id` (nullable, additive): when set, the subject's name, voice and life are the synth's, and the subject's
  `look_words` and references are the synth's film look. `subjectsForNames` keeps working. A saved subject with no synth stays what it
  is today: a look for pictures (a prop, a place, a character that never speaks).
- `made-artist.members[].synth_id` (nullable, additive): a band member who is also a synth, so "the Marsh Lights' drummer" can guest on
  the music round table and remember the album on the next one.
- A household person is never a synth; a synth can be based on one (`based_on.household_member`). The person record does not change.

### 2.5 Making synths

Three ways, all through the existing companion creation screens, which become the synth screens (companion-three-screens; the screens
gain the `based_on` choice for adults and nothing else): by hand; by the show maker, which invents the cast a format needs ("a morning
news anchor" gives a name, pronouns, backstory, dials matched to the part's register, a voice not used by the other cast members, a
portrait through the look path); or by promoting a band member or a saved subject into a synth. Invented synths start at the audience of
the person who made them, clamped by the show's audience (a child's show gets synths with audience `all`). A synth the show maker
invents is listed on the Synths page like any other and can be assigned as a companion ("what did you mean about the rates story?") by
anyone its audience allows.

## 3. A life: continuity across episodes and chat

### 3.1 The record

A life beat is a `memory-record` with `scope: companion`, `companion_id: <synth id>`, `person: null`, `record_kind: memory`, `tier`
`episodic` (most beats) or `durable` (a lasting change: "moved to the coast", "got a dog named Pip"), `category` `event`, `state`,
`relationship` or `fact`, `valid_from` and `valid_to` for anything with a span (an absence), `child_disclosure` set from the synth's
audience (`all` gives `child_ok`, `teen` gives `teen_ok`, `adult` gives `adult_only`), `source` naming what wrote it (`talent.life`,
`talent.episode:<id>`). Every beat passes the output floor at the synth's audience before it is stored, the way a summary does
(rule 10; architect condition 4), so `child_disclosure` is a checked fact, not a copy of the audience. No new table, no new column: `memory_records` already has `companion_id` nullable and
the spec already allows the scope; what is missing is the Home code that reads and writes it (COMP-05 noted the same gap for rapport).
`canRead` stays default-deny for unknown scopes and gains the `companion` branch: readable by anyone who may use the synth
(`mayUseCompanion`) and whose band passes `child_disclosure`.

### 3.2 Who writes beats

1. **Creation.** The synth's `life.seed` becomes the first durable beat, and the show maker adds two or three starting beats that
   fit the backstory (a hobby in progress, a person in their life). The chat model writes them; the person can edit them on the
   synth's page (the memory review list, no new screen).
2. **The daily life tick** (`talent.life.tick`, section 8): for each synth with `life.enabled` that is on air (derived, never stored: cast on at least one show with a schedule; architect condition 17), at most one new beat per
   day, and most days none. The model is given the backstory, the active beats and the date, and asked for one small, plausible, dated
   development or nothing. The prompt names the kinds that keep a life believable (a plan with a date, a small outcome of an earlier
   plan, a passing mood, a person they mention, an absence with a return date about one week in twenty) and forbids real-world claims
   (no real news events, no real places' current conditions, no health scares, no money troubles for a child-audience synth). It is
   the model's judgement, not a schedule of events (rule 1). A beat that contradicts an active one is rejected by the same judge path
   that reconciles memory conflicts (`conflicts_with`), and the day gets no beat.
3. **The show writer, after an episode:** "said on air" beats, so the next episode follows through: "told listeners she is off to the
   coast next week" (valid until the return date), "was away this week; A read the weather" and, on return, "back from the coast, said the
   sea was freezing". The writer gets these as context (section 6.2) and the format's rule says how much to make of them (news: one
   sentence at the top; round table: a short exchange, at most about a minute; debate: nothing).
4. **Not chat.** What a companion says in one person's chat is never written as a shared beat (architect condition 3;
   NO-CROSS-USER-VISIBILITY-01 and COMP-05's own reason: one person's joke would reach another through the companion). A fact that
   comes up in chat stays that pair's rapport (`scope: person`, `companion_id`, COMP-05). Shared beats come only from creation, the
   life tick and the episode writer, and chat reads them (3.3), so the companion and the host still tell one story.

### 3.3 Recall

- **On air:** the show writer receives, per cast member, the active beats (valid now, or no span) ranked by importance and recency,
  capped at eight, plus the show's last three "said on air" beats.
- **In chat:** the turn's recall adds one filter branch beside COMP-05's: `scope = companion AND companion_id = <turn's companion>`,
  capped at four beats, placed in the persona context, not the person's memory block. The companion then says "I'm off to the coast next
  week" in chat and on the podcast, and the person can ask about it in both places.
- **Absences:** when a cast member has an active absence beat on the day an episode is due, the episode is cast without them and the
  writer gets `absent: B (camping, back Tuesday)`. The first episode of the absence mentions it; later ones may not; the return episode
  gets `returned: B` with the absence beat so a brief exchange can happen. A show with every host absent runs its "substitute" rule
  (section 5): a one-host format carries on with a stand-in synth the show maker creates once and keeps; a round table skips the
  episode and the next one mentions the week off.

### 3.4 What a life never does

It never claims a real event happened (the news is the news, the life is fiction), never ages a synth past its audience's floor,
never writes anything about a household member into a synth's life (household facts go into the episode, not the synth), and
never leaks between synths (A knows B is away because the episode writer told A, as a "said on air" beat on the show, not by
reading B's beats).

## 4. Shows and episodes: the Production

### 4.1 A show is a Production

Additive fields on `media-production`, spec first (owner decision 2026-11-04 says the Production is the container for shows):

- `show`: null for a film or a picture production, otherwise `{ format_id, audience, household_facts: bool, sources, schedule,
  length_s, recaps }`.
  - `format_id`: one of section 5's ids.
  - `audience`: `all | teen | adult`, never above the maker's band; every line is floored at it (section 10).
  - `sources`: what the writer may use, by kind: `news` (topics and followed channels, read from the News shelf), `weather` (the
    household's place), `household` (memory the audience may read, lists, reminders; on only when `household_facts`), `documents`
    (Library file ids or Notes the person gave, for the discussion format), `media_item` (a film, book, album or episode to review),
    `bookmarks`. No kind reaches the network; everything is already in the home.
  - `schedule`: `{ every: "1d" | "7d" | null, at: "07:00", days: [..] }`, through the existing scheduler (section 8); null means on
    request only.
  - `length_s`: within the format's range.
  - `recaps`: derived, not stored twice: the last three episodes' "said on air" beats are memory records tagged with the production id
    in `source`.
- `cast[].part` (additive beside `role`): the format part id ("anchor", "host", "co_host", "guest", "moderator", "critic", "dj",
  "narrator", "announcer"). `role` stays the film's free text. `cast[].synth_id` (nullable, additive) is the cast assignment (section 2.3); a household member as
  themselves is a synth with `based_on.household_member`.
- `episodes[]` already exist (id, title, scenes, shots). A spoken episode uses one scene per segment and one shot per line, each shot's
  `voice_line_id` pointing at the rendered clip, so the film tooling's "redo one shot" works for "redo one line" with nothing new. The
  finished recording is a `finished_cut_file_ids` entry, a Library file with `origin: made` and a `generation-provenance` of kind `show`
  (additive enum value) carrying the format, the cast ids, the model and the engines.

### 4.2 An episode in the apps

- A `media-source` of kind `production` (additive enum value, beside root, feed, channel, link, station) with `production_id`, made with
  the show, visible per the show's `shared_to`. Each finished episode becomes a `media-item` under it: title, summary, chapters (one per
  segment), transcript (the final script, so PODCAST-PORT-04's search and PODCAST-PORT-05's Ask work on made episodes), `explicit` and `band_ok`
  from the audience (`adult` gives adult, `teen` gives teen, `all` gives child), duration, the cover (section 7.4). The delivery gate
  never plays an episode to a listener below its `band_ok` (architect condition 1; `explicit` alone would leave a teen show reachable
  by a child). The Podcasts app lists it as a show with a "Made here" badge (the made-radio wording).
- The News app's Listen on Today plays the person's briefing episode when one exists for today (NEWS-04's action; the per-card read-aloud
  stays plain TTS of the card).
- The Media hub's "new across the apps" and search already read media-items; nothing new.

## 5. Formats

A format is a JSON file in the bundled `talent` package (`backend/packages/talent/formats/<id>.json`), checked against a new spec shape
`show-format` (additive): `id`, `label`, `parts[]` (`{ id, label, min, max, register, leads: bool }`), `structure[]` (segment kinds and
their share of the length), `length_s: {min, default, max}`, `talk_over` (`none | light | lively | heated`), `gap_ms`, `sources_allowed`,
`min_audience`, `substitute` (`stand_in | skip`), `stingers: bool`, `first_app`. Household edits to formats are out of scope for v1; a
show picks a format and overrides length, cast and sources only.

## 6. The writer

### 6.1 The brief

Gathered by Home, never by the model: the format; the cast with each synth's persona (dials, interests, backstory, pronouns), their
active life beats and the show's last recaps; the sources: the News shelf's top stories for the show's topics at the audience's band
with NEWS-04's summaries (full text only where the reader already stored it), today's weather summary, household facts the audience may
read, only when `household_facts` and through the memory reads with audience and `sensitive` honoured: a shared show reads `scope:
household` records only; a show that is one person's alone may also read that person's own records, lists and reminders; a shared show
never reads anyone's lists or reminders (architect condition 5; NO-CROSS-USER-VISIBILITY-01), the documents or the item to discuss (chunked by the existing retrieval), the person's name when the
show is theirs. The date, the day of the week and the time of day the episode will air.

### 6.2 Outline, then script

1. **Outline:** one call to the household chat model through the turn engine's completion seam (the path MUSIC-DJ-01 and the film planner
   use; no second model path), with engine-native JSON-schema output (rule 2): `segments[]` of `{ kind, goal, source_refs, seconds,
   leads: part, beats_to_use }`. The outline sizes itself to `length_s` (the legacy outline's arc: hook, intro, body segments, outro).
   A bad outline is repaired once with one more schema-checked call, then the episode is refused in plain words on the show's card.
2. **Script, one call per segment** with a word budget (legacy rule; the budget is the segment's seconds at the part's words per minute,
   about 150 for news, 170 to 190 for a round table): `lines[]` of `{ part, text, delivery, timing }`.
   - `text`: what is said, written spoken (the NAT mechanics: rounding, hedges, evidentials for secondhand facts, short acknowledgements).
   - `delivery`: `{ energy: low | mid | high, pace: slow | mid | quick, tags: [...] }`; tags are the engine's own sound tags
     (`[laugh]`, `[sigh]`, ...) and are stripped where the engine lacks them (today's `tts.ts` rule).
   - `timing`: `{ gap_ms, overlap_ms, cut_in_at_word, talked_over: yield | continue, backchannels: [{ part, word_index, text }] }`,
     within the format's `talk_over` budget (section 7.3 says what each does).
   The persona of each part comes from `composePersonaPrompt` for that synth (rule 14's build note for the DJ: the companion
   personality path, never new prose), adapted to the audience band with `adaptPersonaToBand`. A part's register is dial overrides
   (formality, filler_density, hedging, clock_style, the social dials) applied through that same path, as section 5's table states
   them, never a prose block of its own (rule 14; architect condition 8). The backstory and the beats are in the context, not the
   prompt prose.
3. **Checks per line** (section 10), then the script is the episode's transcript.

### 6.3 Writer rules (ported from the legacy `script.ts` and the sample films, then extended)

1. Use only the supplied facts. A fact not in the brief is not said; opinion is fine and is marked as the synth's.
2. Outside voices are quoted as such ("the mayor said", "the paper reports"), never performed. A real person in the news is talked about,
   not imitated, unless they are cast (section 10).
3. No assistant phrasings, no "as an AI", no plugs for Home, no sign-off that is not the show's own.
4. Reactions are short: a real interjection is one to six words. At most one interruption per segment in `light`, two in `lively`, no
   cap but a fairness rule in `heated` (each side gets equal floor time, the moderator restores it).
5. Continuity: a "said on air" beat is followed through once, briefly, and then dropped; an absence is mentioned in the first episode
   and on the return, not in between.
6. Household facts are mentioned as a local station would: once, warmly, never with a detail the audience may not know (the memory read
   already excluded them), never a child's sensitive record.
7. The register is the part's, the personality is the synth's: an anchor who is funny in chat is dry on air and lets one wry line
   through in the outro.
8. The last line of an episode is the next one's hook when the show has a schedule ("tomorrow, the rates decision").

## 7. The studio: voices, lines, overlap, assembly

### 7.1 Voice casting

A synth's voice is its `companion.voice.<id>` setting, resolved per request (rule 14). When the show maker invents a synth it
picks a voice the other cast members are not using (the legacy `voicePool` rule, kept per show instead of round-robin), from: the
Pocket presets (26), the Chatterbox built-in and the VOICES-02 starter set when it ships, and the household's own clips (owner or admin
uploaded). Two cast members on the same engine preset get a `pitch_semitones` offset of two to three so they stay apart. A show whose
audience is `all` or `teen` casts preset voices only: a child or teen never hears a voice made from a clip (SAFETY, as written). The
synth's page shows the voice through the existing voice picker; changing it there changes the next episode, not the old ones.

### 7.2 Rendering lines

One clip per line through the Stack's speech engine. Today Home calls `/v1/audio/speech` synchronously for chat; an episode has fifty
to three hundred lines, so the lines go as one batch job of the spec's existing `media.tts` kind (TALENT-03 builds the Stack handler:
input `lines[] { id, voice, text, tags, pitch_semitones }`, output one wav per line with its measured duration and a sha256), in the
Stack's lowest queue class and the household's resource order (chat first, then pictures, then video, then songs, then shows). Each
line's text goes through the normalizer and REG-02's voice cues first, in Home. The engine's own tags carry the delivery where it
supports them; `energy` and `pace` map to the engine's temperature and, when an engine gains a speed control, to that; until then pace
is written into the words (shorter sentences read quicker) and never into a post-process time stretch (it shifts pitch).

### 7.3 Overlap and interruptions without timestamps

No TTS path returns word times, and the design does not need them:

- **An interruption** is scripted at a word: the interrupted line is rendered as two clips, the words before `cut_in_at_word` and the
  words after. The interrupter's clip starts at the end of the first clip minus `overlap_ms` (250 to 700 ms, the format's `talk_over`
  sets the band). With `talked_over: yield` the second clip is dropped (the speaker gives way; the script wrote the first part to trail
  naturally). With `continue` the second clip plays under the interrupter at -6 dB for at most 1.5 s and then stops (talked over), and
  the script gives the interrupted speaker a short "as I was saying" line later if the writer chose to.
- **A backchannel** ("mhm", "right", "no way") is rendered once per synth and reused; it is laid under the long line at the
  proportional position of `word_index` (word count over duration; good enough for a grunt) at -10 dB.
- **Gaps:** `gap_ms` per format (news 350, round table 120 to 250, debate 0 to 120, kids 300) and per line override. The legacy 0.18 s
  is the round-table default; the legacy speed floor of 1.2 is dropped (speed is the listener's `audio.speed.podcast` setting).
- **Laughs and sighs** are the engine's tags inside the line where it has them, else a short rendered "ha" clip under the next line's
  start, never a stock sound.

The result is an edit list: `{ clip, start_ms, gain_db, fade_in_ms, fade_out_ms }` for every rendered clip.

### 7.4 Assembly

One Stack `media.edit` job: the edit list as `concat` and `audio_overlay` ops with offsets and ducking, the show's intro and outro
stingers (four to eight seconds, made once per show by the music engine from the format's brief, with fades, after the lines so no two
engines wait on each other), a music bed under the intro and outro only (ducked under speech), loudness to -16 LUFS, export as MP3 (the
Podcasts player's format) with chapter marks from the segments. The cover: one picture job from the show's title and cast portraits
(the companion look path), made at show creation, reused per episode with the episode title; a square and a 16:9.

### 7.5 What to bench before TALENT-05 (the first multi-voice slice)

TALENT-BENCH-01, on the same 90-second round-table script: (a) per-line rendering with the scripted overlap of 7.3; (b) the same lines
rendered with no overlap and 180 ms gaps; (c) the Stack's recorded speech (Drama 3, four speakers from reference clips) given the whole
exchange. Judged by ear by Jesse and two listeners: which sounds like people in a room. Measured: render seconds per spoken minute, memory
peak, and whether the floor check can read Drama 3's output (it has no per-line text to check, so it would need the transcript path).
Drama 3 can only ever serve adult-audience shows (made voices). The bench decides whether 7.3 is the base path for adults too.

## 8. Scheduling, freshness and resources

- `talent.shows.due` runs every 15 minutes in `CORE_JOBS`: for each show with a schedule, when the local day has passed `at` and no
  episode exists for that day (the legacy once-per-local-day rule), it queues the episode at the lowest class. The morning briefing is
  queued at `at` minus the show's measured make time so it is ready at `at`; a late one is still made and marked with its real time.
- `talent.life.tick` runs daily after midnight local time for every synth with `life.enabled` that is on air (cast on a scheduled show) (section 3.2).
- A listener never waits in front of an empty player: a show with no episode for today shows yesterday's and "Making today's episode"
  through the existing "one job's progress" S6 row; no new status surface.
- Resource tiers (TIER-SPEC-01): Reduced makes no scheduled shows (on request only, formats up to 8 minutes); Standard one scheduled
  show per day; Boost and Max more, with the number set by the measured render time. Numbers to measure in TALENT-BENCH-01 before the
  defaults are written: TTS render seconds per spoken minute on each engine, model seconds per script minute.
- Order: chat, pictures, video, songs, then shows (owner order 2026-10-21). A show job yields to a person's own job between lines.

## 10. Safety, privacy and real people

- **Floor:** a show's `audience` is at most the maker's band. Every line passes `evaluateReply` at that band as text and as speech
  (rule 10: a child's floor applies to every sentence); a failing line is rewritten once within the segment call, then cut; a segment
  that cannot pass is dropped and the outline re-sized; an episode with no passing segment is withheld with a plain reason on the card.
  Adults get the CSAM-only check (rule 0's owner ruling). The episode's media-item carries `explicit` for adult audiences and the
  Podcasts child gate hides it as it hides any explicit episode.
- **Who hears:** the show's `shared_to`, through the Production's own sharing; a child's show lives in the child's library; member
  sharing comes with MEDIA-SHARE-01 and changes nothing here.
- **Real people (owner, 2026-11-07: any, within the standing rules):** an adult may cast a real public figure or a household member;
  the model decides what that synth says (rule 0, SAFETY: an adult may make media of any real or public figure). A real public
  figure is a synth with `based_on.real_person` (its subject's `real` follows) and no made voice unless the household uploaded a clip; a household member is
  cast as a synth based on them, with their own household clone. Minors' shows cast invented synths and household members only (SAFETY: a teen
  or child never gets a real person outside the household) and preset voices only. The synth page says "Real person" where it
  applies, as the subject does. Nothing here adds a consent prompt (LIKENESS-SIMPLIFY-01 stands).
- **Nothing leaves the home:** the brief is built from what Home already fetched (the News shelf under its own budgets, the weather
  summary, memory, files); the writer runs on the household model; the voices on the Stack. No source kind opens a connection.
- **Logs** carry counts and milliseconds, never a line (rule 6a). The script is kept only as the episode's transcript under the
  episode's own access.
- **Memory:** life beats of a synth are not a person's memory: "forget everything about me" does not touch them; deleting the
  synth deletes its beats (as COMP-05 deletes rapport). A beat is never written from a guest turn.

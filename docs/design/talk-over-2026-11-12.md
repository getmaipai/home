# Talk before you finish: the end-of-turn acknowledgement with a protected overlap (TALK-OVER-01, design note, 2026-11-12)

Owner's ask (Jesse, 2026-11-12): "it's still not talking over me which I don't like; it should start talking over me before I
finish to make it sound natural." Same day, relayed through the coordinator: the early sound is provisional until the final
transcript confirms the person stopped; a protected overlap window of about 800 ms (a setting, 500 to 1200 ms, adaptive) during
which the person's voice does not stop Home; one decision at the window's end (still talking: a hard stop with only a 10 to 20 ms
anti-click ramp, the answer discarded, nothing said, the speech merged into the same turn; stopped: Home continues and the final
confirms); the first early piece short; echo never counted as the person; two wrong early starts in one conversation drop the
level a step; the STT keeps transcribing through the overlap, with word accuracy on overlapped audio measured.

Read for this: `RULES.md` (rules 0, 9 with its held-resume-session and talker-first gaps, 10, 13, S6),
`speculative-start-2026-11-11.md` (the held run, the edge partial, the measured 1.40 s), `full-duplex-voice-2026-10-24.md`
section 3b (continuers and acknowledgements, the fixed clip pool and its gate conditions), `talker-first-2026-11-12.md`,
`sttSession.ts`, `speculation.ts`, `routes/turn.ts`, `liveVoiceSession.tsx`, `sentenceSpeechScheduler.ts`,

## Supersedes

- `full-duplex-voice-2026-10-24.md` section 3b item 5 ("never over the person's voice"), **for the end-of-turn acknowledgement
  only**: Jesse's ruling of 2026-11-12 is a protected overlap window in which the clip plays on while the person may still be
  talking. Continuers in a mid-sentence pause (DUPLEX-07, not built) keep item 5 as written.
- Section 3b item 1's "cached on disk" and "volume 0.6": the acknowledgement clips are rendered once per session and voice and
  kept in memory (nothing new on disk), played at 0.8 on the reply's own playback bus so the level meter and the audio session
  see one source. Section 3b's person-scope `person.voice` on/off key is **not** replaced: the level is a household admin setting
  (`voice.talk_over`), and a person's own off switch arrives with DUPLEX-07's key when that lands.
- The held run's quick cue (VOICE-QUICK-01's `ACK`/`REACT`, `routes/turn.ts`) is skipped on a turn whose clip played, so one
  sound is heard, not two. The thinking and "still on it" cues stay.
- `liveVoiceSession.tsx`'s rule that a sound never pauses the voice before 700 ms (`DUPLEX_CONFIRM_MS`, Jesse 2026-10-30) stands:
  the clip is never cut at the first sound, only at the window's end (800 ms or more).
- `SentenceSpeechScheduler.stop()` cut sources with no ramp; it now ramps the bus to zero in 15 ms first (an anti-click ramp, too
  short to hear). The pause and resume path is unchanged. The hard stop is for answer audio only.
- Section 2 item 4's "the clip, if still playing, is cut with a hard stop" is **replaced** (Jesse, 2026-11-12, after hearing
  "blips like half a syllable"): once the acknowledgement clip starts it always plays to its end. Nothing cuts it: not the person's
  voice, a `resumed` event, a newer partial, a held run replaced or discarded, nor the reply scheduler's stop; the one exception is
  the call closing. The protected window decides only whether the held answer is released or discarded. The clip is short (a render
  over 700 ms is left out of the pool), starts only on a stable edge (the hub's 0.2 s rule), and never twice in one utterance. The
  record carries `clip_started_ms`, `clip_ended_ms` (both against the end of speech) and `clip_completed`.
- `talker-first-2026-11-12.md`: when its opener lands, a clip turn already opened with a sound; that build decides whether the
  opener is skipped after a clip (recommended: skip, so one sound) or the clip is skipped when an opener is expected. Until then
  the clip is the opening sound and the opener does not exist.

## 1. What the numbers allow

From the person's last voiced sample, on the live hub and the bench (`speculative-start-2026-11-11.md`): the VAD's first silent
frame about 50 ms later; the edge partial's transcript 150 to 250 ms later; the held run's engine first token 0.8 s after that (0.5 s
of it the volatile prompt tail), its first sentence about 1.0 s after; the final at 0.82 s of silence; the browser's TTS first bytes
0.3 to 0.4 s; decode and audio start 0.1 to 0.2 s. So the answer's first clause cannot sound before about 1.3 s after the person
stops, whatever is held or released: the final (0.82 s) lands before the clause exists, so releasing the clause before the final
would gain nothing. The only sound that can start within the overlap is content-free, and it must already be rendered: a short
acknowledgement in Home's own voice, played from memory the moment the hub's edge partial reads as a finished sentence. That is what
this note builds. The answer follows through the confirmed held run as today.

## 2. Mechanism

1. **End-of-turn predictor on the hub** (`backend/src/lib/turnEnd.ts`, pure). At the edge partial (the acoustic cue: the voice
   just stopped) the hub scores the text for completeness and stamps the partial with `end: { score, level }`, where `level` is the
   lowest setting level at which this partial counts as finished (`none`, `gentle`, `natural`). Cues, in order: a trailing
   connective, article, preposition, auxiliary or filler ("and", "to", "the", "uh", "I mean") makes it unfinished whatever the
   punctuation (the recognizer adds a period to any segment); a trailing comma or ellipsis is unfinished; terminal punctuation
   (`.` `?` `!`) and a question or request shape (a wh-word, an auxiliary or an imperative verb up front, three words or more) raise the
   score; a bare trailing number without a unit lowers it ("set a timer for ten"); one or two words are ambiguous. Gentle fires at
   0.85 (terminal punctuation plus a clean ending), natural at 0.5 (a plain unpunctuated statement that reads complete). No model
   call: the Stack's Smart Turn (DUPLEX-02) can replace the shape rules later behind the same `end` field once it is measured. Rule 1
   is not touched: this decides when the person is done, never whether a message needs a search or a tool.
2. **The early piece: one acknowledgement clip.** The pool is one code constant on the hub, `ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_CLIPS` in
   `replyVariation.ts` beside `THINKING_CUE_VARIANTS` ("Mm-hm.", "Okay.", "Hmm.", "Right."; DUPLEX-07's pool, so that item adds
   its groups to the same constant), sent to the client in `ready.talk_over.clips`, each through `normalizeForSpeech`. A unit test
   runs the output floor (`evaluateSafety`) over every entry for every band (rule 10, section 3b item 4), although only adults ever
   hear one. At `ready`, when the level is on, the browser renders the pool through `POST /api/tts` in the voice the reply will use
   (the same `voice` the scheduler sends for the selected speaker, so the hub resolves voice, engine and companion as for any
   sentence: rule 14), keyed by that voice id, rendered again when the speaker changes, kept in memory for the session. On an edge
   partial whose `end.level` is at or below the setting, and only while a held run is being started for it, the clip plays at once
   through the same playback bus the reply uses (never the same clip twice in a row). It is a spoken cue: never written into the
   conversation, never shown, never the reply. The held run's own quick cue is skipped on that turn so the person hears one sound,
   not two. First sound: the edge partial's arrival plus about 30 ms (est.; measured in section 5).
3. **The protected overlap window.** From the clip's start, `window_ms` (`voice.talk_over_window_ms`, 500 to 1200, default 800; the
   effective window is never shorter than the clip plus 250 ms). Inside it nothing the person says stops the clip or discards the
   held run. The hub keeps listening and transcribing exactly as today: the person's resumed words are the same utterance, because
   its silence timer has not run out, so the final carries everything they said. Nothing is lost by construction.
4. **One decision at the window's end** (the client, which holds the clip and the held run's ids):
   - the final already arrived and matched: `confirmed`; the content follows through the confirmed held run as today;
   - the hub reported the person speaking again (below) and no later edge partial has arrived: `continued`; the clip, if still
     playing, is cut with a hard stop (gain to zero in 15 ms, then the source stops: no audible fade), the held run is discarded
     (`discard: true`, stored nowhere, no tool ran), nothing is said, and the normal turn answers the whole utterance when the final
     lands. Counted as a wrong start;
   - neither: wait for the final, which confirms on a match or, when it differs, discards and counts a wrong start.
   After a confirmation the normal barge-in applies (the existing pause, resume and `interrupt()` path): a different code path,
   named apart in the timing record and the tests. `SentenceSpeechScheduler.stop()` gains the same 15 ms anti-click ramp so a
   barge-in after confirmation is a hard stop too (Jesse: people do not fade when talking).
5. **"Speaking again" is the hub's call, and echo never counts.** The hub, which has the frames, sends `vad { speaking: true,
   resumed: true, voiced_ms }` once when voiced frames after an edge partial have lasted 200 ms (Silero at 0.5/0.35 behind the RMS
   pre-gate, so breath, a cough or room noise does not reach 200 ms of voiced frames). The browser's echo cancellation stays on
   (`mic-capture.ts`; DUPLEX-00 measured Home's own voice at 6.6 dB below room noise in desktop Chrome with it on). On top, the
   client counts a resume only when it reaches past the clip's own playback plus 100 ms, or a later partial has more words than the
   held text: a resume that fits inside the clip's playback is treated as echo and ignored. No voiceprint (DUPLEX-08 is its own design).
6. **Back-off per conversation.** Two wrong starts in one conversation drop the level one step for the rest of it (natural to
   gentle to off), and every wrong start widens the window 100 ms (cap 1200). Client memory, keyed by the conversation id; a person
   whose mid-sentence pauses read as finished gets a longer hold within minutes. Per-person persistence across conversations would
   need a person-scope key and is a follow-up, not built here.
7. **Who gets it (rule 0).** Adults only, signed in on their own chat or phone surface, never a minor, a robot device or an unknown
   speaker: the hub decides the level per session from the signed-in person and sends it in `ready` (`talk_over: { level,
   window_ms }`); a session that qualifies for no level gets no field and the client has nothing to play. It rides on the held run, so
   `voice.speculative_start` off means off. `speculation.ts`'s refusals stay as they are. The clip plays before the turn's safety
   path has run; the hub stamps `end.level: "none"` when the deterministic safety floor flags the partial (`evaluateSafety` for the
   adult band: the CSAM detector and the crisis signal), so no acknowledgement follows a crisis disclosure (DUPLEX-07 section 3b
   item 4).
8. **The setting.** `voice.talk_over` (household, admin, advanced, select: off, gentle, natural), label "Talk before I finish".
   Help in plain words: with gentle, Home says a short "mm-hm" the moment a finished sentence ends, and the answer follows; natural
   also does it on plain statements, so it happens more and is wrong more; if you keep talking Home goes quiet and answers all of it;
   adults only. `voice.talk_over_window_ms` (household, admin, advanced, number 500 to 1200, default 800): how long Home keeps going
   while you both talk before it decides. No memory or resource words anywhere a person reads.

## 3. What stays inside the rules, and what would not

Everything above keeps rule 9's held-resume-session line as written: the held run's own words reach nobody before the exact
match; a discarded run stores nothing and runs no tool; minors, robots and unconfirmed speakers never start one. The early sound
is a spoken cue from a closed list (the named gap "sentence-gated speech scheduling with the spoken cue", with DUPLEX-07 section
3b's conditions), never the run's text. Two things this note does **not** build, because each breaks a clause of that line, and only
Jesse changes a rule:

- Speaking the held run's first clause before the final: "held and confirmed only on an exact trimmed match with the final words".
  Section 1 shows it would buy nothing today (the clause does not exist when the final lands), so it is not asked for.
- Starting the held run on a cadence partial while the person is still voicing, when the text already reads complete: "begun
  privately on the edge partial transcript". It is the one lever that could put the answer itself before the end of speech (up to
  about 0.5 s, est.) and would cost two whole-buffer decodes a second on the shared STT plus a wasted model run on every wrong
  guess. If Jesse wants it, the line would change to "begun privately on a partial transcript the end-of-turn predictor reads as
  finished", with the same confirm-on-exact-match and discard rules.

## 4. Wire, record and tests

Additive fields only (a client that does not know them ignores them; the `@maipai/spec` `SttWireEvent` is unchanged, as with
`edge: true`): `ready.talk_over`, `partial.end`, `vad.resumed` and `vad.voiced_ms`. The voice timing message and record gain

used), `decision` (`confirmed`, `continued`, `none`) and `yield_ms` (hard-stop latency: the decision to the gain reaching zero), all
counts (rule 6a). `bun run voice:latency` prints them.

Tests that fail before the build: `turnEnd.test.ts` (a labelled set of 34 utterances: finished questions and requests, mid-sentence
pauses such as "I want to, uh" and "and then I", trailing "and", bare numbers, one-word answers, with the expected level each;

`resumed` is sent once after 200 ms of voice following the edge and not for 100 ms of sound); `talkOver.test.ts` on the client state
machine (still talking at the window end stops and discards; stopped at the window end continues and the final confirms; a resume

## 5. Bench and default

`backend/scripts/bench/voice-first-sound.ts --talk-over gentle|natural` adds, per turn: `early_release_ms` (the clip's cue against
the end of the played speech), whether the predictor fired, the decision and the hard-stop latency; the utterance set gains
mid-sentence pauses spliced at a clause boundary (300, 500 and 650 ms of silence, then the rest) so the wrong-start rate is measured
on the cases that cause it. A second pass mixes a rendered acknowledgement under the utterance's tail at 0 dB (no echo cancellation
at all) and at minus 6 dB (the measured residual with it on) and reports the final's word accuracy against the script. Numbers per
level are recorded below when the run is done. The default is `gentle` only if its wrong-start rate on the labelled set is 10 percent
or under, else `off`.

## Measured

**Speculative start, made to fire (2026-11-12).** The feature was on by default but fired on no live turn. Three causes, each
fixed with a test: (1) Home's registry keeps the spec's copy of any key the spec already carries, so the 2026-11-12 "default on"
for `voice.speculative_start` never took effect and the hub refused every start as "Speculative start is off" (fixed spec first,
spec-v0.1.370, pinned); (2) the recognizer's voice detector goes silent between words, so a two-second question produced ten edge
partials, each started a held run on a fragment, and the burst of refused requests hit the turn budget (429): the hub now marks a
partial `edge` only after 0.2 s of silence and scores it, the client starts one run per edge, replaces it on newer words, never on
a plainly unfinished fragment, and stops after an "off"; (3) the browser's own handler had discarded every held run (the edge
partial counted as resumed speech; fixed earlier the same day). Verified in the built-in browser against a dev hub with a fake
microphone: one edge partial, start, the final matched, confirmed, first sound 0.93 s after the final (3.4 s before). The record's
`speculative` field now reads off / armed / no_conversation / too_long / no_edge / unfinished / late_start / refused /
request_failed / started / confirmed / discarded, and `bun run voice:latency` splits the first sound by it.

**Predictor, after the first live day.** Eight live turns in a row started no run because their last word ("it", "on", "you",
"do") sat in a broad dangling-word list; narrowed to words that practically never close a spoken sentence, the labelled set
passes with the sentence-final cases added.

**Slow warm turns.** The stream route never aborted a conversation warm still in flight (only the non-stream and speech routes
did), so a turn within a second of the last reply waited for the engine to finish reading the warm's prompt; and the memory judge
treated a call pause over 5 s as idle. Both fixed: the warm is aborted and `prewarm_in_flight` is stamped on the record, and every
voiced onset or final of a listening session gates background model work for a minute (LISTEN-GATE-01).

**What `early_release_ms` means, and why no correct start is negative (first live day, 2026-11-12).** It is the clip's start
against the end of the whole utterance (the final's arrival minus the hub's `speech_end_to_final_ms`). A correct start is always
positive, about +0.25 to +0.45 s: the hub marks the edge partial only after 0.2 s of silence (the voice detector goes silent between
words, so an earlier mark fired on fragments), plus the transcription. A large negative value (one live row: -4.2 s, decision
`continued`) is a wrong start: the clip played in a mid-sentence pause that long before the person finished. So the clip never
sounds "before you finish" on a correct turn; it sounds in the gap a listener leaves, and the only clips that overlap the person's
voice are the wrong starts, which Jesse heard as "blips like half a syllable". To sound earlier the clip would have to fire on a
cadence partial while the voice is still going, which is exactly a guess at the end, and every miss is such a blip. The record now
says per turn how many edge partials the hub marked (`edge_partials`), whether a clip was cut, when and why (`clip_cut_ms`,
`cut_reason`), and a clip is never cut in its first 300 ms nor at the final: only the window's end cuts, and only when the person is
clearly still speaking. A silent speculative path is labelled `busy_thinking` / `busy_speaking` / `busy_connecting` / `muted` when
the edge partial came while the session was not listening.

**Talk-over numbers** (early-release time, wrong-start rate per level and gap, word accuracy under the clip): run
`backend/scripts/bench/talk-over.ts` on a dev hub; filled in when it has run. The setting stays `off` until the bench's
wrong-start rate at gentle is 10 percent or under. The acknowledgement clip needs a real microphone to confirm end to end (the
overlap, the hard-stop feel, the echo gate with the browser's AEC): headless audio cannot exercise it.

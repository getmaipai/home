# Wikipedia

The Wikipedia app reads Wikipedia from a copy stored on your Home computer. Nothing goes to the internet when you read, and it works when the internet is down.

## Get Wikipedia first

An owner or admin has to download it once. Open **Settings** and choose **Offline data**. Switch on the Wikipedia version you want, then choose **Apply**:

- Everything, with pictures: the biggest download.
- Everything, no pictures: smaller.
- Short introductions only: the smallest.

If it is not downloaded yet, the Wikipedia app says so and links to that page. Children and teens only see books made for them.

## Find and read

Open **Wikipedia** from the apps list. Type in the search box. Titles appear as you type. Press Enter, or **Command K** on a Mac or **Control K** on Windows, to jump to the search box from anywhere in the app.

- **Home** lists the articles you opened lately. The list clears when you close the tab.
- **Explore** shows a few articles to wander into.
- **Random article** opens one at a venture.

An article shows a title, a short description, and a bold first sentence. A facts box and a Contents list sit on the right on a wide screen. On a phone, long sections fold up: tap a heading to open it. Rest on a link to see a small preview.

## Save an article

Press **Save** on an article. Your saved articles are under **Saved**. Press **Remove** there to take one off. Saved lists are private to each person.

## Keep an article on this device

Press **Keep on this device** at the bottom of an article. It opens with no connection to Home. Find kept articles under **Offline**. Kept copies live in this browser only, and Chrome and Edge can keep them. On an iPhone or iPad, use the MaiPai Go app. Signing out removes them. More about keeping copies is in [Keep copies on this device](keep-on-this-device.md).

## If something goes wrong

- "Wikipedia is not in your library yet": ask an owner or admin to download it.
- A Save or Keep that fails shows a message. Check your connection to Home and try again.
- An article that a child or teen may not read does not open for them.

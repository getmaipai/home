# Kids and media

Child controls show the home library and channels a grown-up picked for that child. Items must also pass the child safety
checks. Songs marked explicit stay hidden with both Child and Teen controls, even if you change the source list.

An owner or admin can change a person's approved sources rule and daily media minutes. A child or teen cannot change these
rules. The daily limit uses one total across media apps and screens. Set it to 0 for no limit. The total starts fresh at local
midnight on the Home computer.

Music and audiobooks count too. An owner or admin can turn on the home rule that leaves them out of the total. It starts off.
Podcasts still count.

When screen time runs out, Home keeps the last place and the player pauses within about 15 seconds, even if the video has already
loaded. If the app is in the background, it can take up to a minute. Pressing play again pauses it right away (a quick tap may play for a moment first). Opening a video or song after time is up shows the words too. The child sees these
words once:

> Screen time is done for today.
> Ask a grown-up for more.

When time runs out, the video or song file stops loading too, so a child cannot keep watching by opening the file directly.

The home admins get one notice a day, for a child or a teen who has a time limit. It names the person whose time ran out. It
does not say what they watched, heard or read. An owner or admin can add minutes for that day. Extra minutes end at midnight.

A child or teen with a time limit cannot keep media for offline use, because the Home computer cannot count time it cannot see.

This works in every media app: Videos, Music, Podcasts and the small player at the bottom of the screen. Anywhere else,
the file itself stops loading. A grown-up's own limit is only a reminder and never pauses their player. If a grown-up adds
minutes, the next press of play works again. There is no new parent screen yet. Allowed hours and a button to ask a grown-up are still to come.
